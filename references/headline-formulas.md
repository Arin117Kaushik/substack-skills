# Headline Formulas

> Ideas adapted from the public Substack Starter Kit by Write Build Scale, rewritten in our own words. Changes: every formula that implies evidence or a guarantee now requires the user's real result.

A headline has three jobs: stir curiosity, state the value, and fit the reader. Draft 3 options from different formulas, name the formula beside each, and keep each at 60 characters or fewer.

## The eight formulas

| # | Formula | Shape | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Number list | `{N} ways to {outcome} without {pain}` | The post is a list the reader can skim |
| 2 | How-to | `How to {outcome} even if {obstacle}` | The post teaches a method |
| 3 | Question | `{Struggle}? Here's how to {result}` | The reader will recognise the struggle |
| 4 | Curiosity gap | `The {overlooked thing} behind {result}` | The post reveals something non-obvious, and delivers it |
| 5 | Emotion | `{Feeling} {topic} that {changes how the reader sees X}` | The user's own feeling about the topic is strong and true |
| 6 | Strong opinion | `Why {popular belief} is wrong` | The user actually holds the opposing view |
| 7 | Data | `{Their number} {what it showed}` | The user ran the numbers, or a named source is linked |
| 8 | Specific result | `{Action} that {result} in {timeframe}` | The user got that result in that time |

Combining two works: a how-to with an obstacle, or an opinion with a number.

## Guardrails (these override the formulas)

- **Formulas 7 and 8 need the user's real data.** "New data shows", "research proves", "guaranteed", "proven" and "in 7 days" are claims. No user number or named source means use another formula (`references/anti-fabrication.md`).
- **A curiosity gap must pay off.** If the post doesn't deliver the hidden thing in its first third, pick another formula.
- **No fake drama.** Emotion words stay in proportion to the post ("terrifying", "shocking" and "mind-blowing" fail `references/voice-rules.md` rule 6).
- **No dashes as connectors** in the headline (rule 1). Use a colon or a comma.

## Audit every option

1. Does it make me curious?
2. Does it promise a clear value?
3. Does it use a strong, specific word rather than a vague one?
4. Does it fit what this publication's readers care about?
5. Is it 60 characters or fewer, and does the post keep its promise?

Show the audit as one line per option, then name the pick and why.
